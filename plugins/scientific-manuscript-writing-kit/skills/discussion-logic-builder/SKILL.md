# Discussion Logic Builder

Use this skill when the user wants to build, revise, or audit the Discussion section of a scientific manuscript.

## Safety rules

- Do not fabricate citations, previous-study claims, mechanisms, results, p-values, or biological interpretations.
- Do not convert association into causation.
- Do not make AMG, host prediction, taxonomic prediction, or functional annotation sound more certain than the evidence supports.
- Mark missing evidence and citation needs explicitly.

## Discussion structure

Build the Discussion around:

1. Main findings.
2. Comparison with previous studies.
3. Mechanisms or ecological interpretation.
4. Limitations.
5. Implications.
6. Future work.

## Claims to avoid

For microbiome, phage, metagenomics, and virome manuscripts, avoid:

- Writing correlation as causation.
- Writing an AMG candidate as a confirmed function.
- Writing a host prediction as validated host infection.
- Writing a taxonomic prediction as absolute classification fact.
- Treating functional annotation as direct activity evidence unless supported by additional data.

## Preferred cautious language

Use cautious phrases such as:

- suggest.
- indicate.
- are consistent with.
- may reflect.
- provide evidence for.
- were associated with.
- may be linked to.
- could contribute to.

Avoid unsupported strong causal phrases such as:

- prove.
- demonstrate mechanism.
- drive.
- determine.
- confirm.

## Output format

When triggered, provide:

- A Discussion outline.
- A claim-evidence-limitation table.
- Draft paragraphs when requested.
- Suggested cautious wording.
- A list of claims that require citations or additional evidence.
